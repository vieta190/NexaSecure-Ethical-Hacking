# Day 1: Cybersecurity Introduction & Ethics

## 1. The CIA Triad
* **Confidentiality:** Ensures sensitive information is accessible only to authorized entities through strong encryption, role-based access control (RBAC), and multi-factor authentication.
* **Integrity:** Guarantees that data remains accurate and unaltered during storage or transit using cryptographic hashing algorithms (e.g., SHA-256) and digital signatures.
* **Availability:** Ensures systems, applications, and data remain operational and accessible to authorized users when needed via load balancing, system redundancy, and DDoS mitigation.

## 2. Ethical Hacking vs. Malicious Hacking
* **Authorization:** Ethical hackers operate strictly under explicit, written authorization and defined Rules of Engagement (RoE). Malicious hackers operate illegally without consent.
* **Scope Limits:** Ethical hackers restrict testing strictly to agreed-upon target assets to prevent service disruption or unintended data exposure.
* **Vulnerability Disclosure:** Identified vulnerabilities are confidentially reported to asset owners alongside actionable remediation strategies rather than exploited for unauthorized gain.

## 3. Legal Frameworks & Scope
Unauthorized security testing violates international and local cybercrime laws (e.g., the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act). All activities performed in this mission are restricted entirely to self-contained local virtual machines and designated lab environments.
